2000 Jeep Grand Cherokee (WJ) · 4.0L inline-six · 4WD

Finding why the scan tool can't talk to the engine computer

Your scan tool calls it the ECM. Jeep calls it the PCM (Powertrain Control Module). Work the flow top to bottom. Every box tells you what to do, what reading you should get, and where to go next.

The computerPCM, passenger-side firewall in the engine bay. Three 32-pin plugs: black (C1), white (C2), gray (C3).
The portData link connector (DLC), under the dash to the left of the steering column, near the hood release.
The data wireOne wire called the PCI bus (protocol SAE J1850 VPW). Pin 2 of the port. Yellow with a violet stripe.
Who shares that wirePCM, gauge cluster, ABS, airbag module, radio, overhead console, Sentry Key module, and more. One bad one can silence all of them.
Overview

The whole flow on one page

Two things you can observe without any tool decide most of the diagnosis: what the odometer shows with the key on, and whether the engine runs. The five lettered branches below are worked out in detail further down.

What you need

  • A digital multimeter (volts and ohms). A test light helps for fuses.
  • A second scan tool if you can borrow one, ideally a wired handheld rather than a cheap Bluetooth dongle.
  • The fuse maps printed on the lids of both fuse boxes. Fuse numbers below come from WJ fuse charts; the lid on your truck wins if it differs.
  • A paperclip or back-probe pins for reaching into connectors without damaging them.

Safety

  • Disconnect the battery negative cable before unplugging the PCM or any module. Never unplug the PCM with the key on.
  • After disconnecting the battery, wait two full minutes before touching the airbag module under the center console.
  • Never probe port pin 16 with an ohmmeter while the battery is connected, and never let a probe bridge pin 16 to pin 2. That is the classic way to blow the port fuse and silence the data wire.
Start here

Step 0 · Rule out the easy stuff

Five minutes. Most "no communication" calls end right here.

0.1

Is the key in RUN (not ACC) and is the battery healthy?

Measure across the battery posts with everything off. The PCM goes quiet below roughly 10 volts, and a cheap scanner will give up before that.

Battery, key off: 12.4 V or more is good. 12.0 V or less: charge it before doing anything else.
  • LOWCharge or jump the battery, then retry the scan tool. If it connects, you are done.
  • GOODContinue to 0.2.
0.2

Does this scan tool work on a different car?

Plug it into any other OBD-II vehicle. If it fails there too, the tool or its cable is the problem, not the Jeep. If you are using a phone app with a Bluetooth plug, set the app's protocol by hand to J1850 VPW (listed as protocol 2 in most ELM327 apps) and try again. Many bargain plugs handle this older Chrysler protocol badly on auto-detect.

  • FAILS ELSEWHEREReplace or repair the tool. Done.
  • WORKS ELSEWHEREContinue to 0.3.
0.3

Did anything change right before this started?

A stereo, alarm or remote-start install, a jump start done backwards, a dead cigarette lighter, or someone else's scanner plugged in. Each one points at a specific place in the flow: aftermarket wiring usually taps the ignition feed or the data wire, a backwards jump usually kills fuses or the PCM, and a dead lighter or a borrowed scanner points at the port fuse.

  • ANYNote it. Look there first when you reach that branch.
  • NEXTGo to the first big question, 1.1.
1.1 · The question that splits the job

Turn the key to RUN, wait 20 seconds. Does the odometer window say "no bus"?

The gauge cluster listens to the PCM on the same data wire your scanner uses. If it stops hearing the PCM, it replaces the mileage with "no bus" (it looks like "no bUS"). Also note whether the gauges sweep at key-on and whether the Check Engine light comes on for a few seconds and then goes out.

  • NO, odometer normalThe PCM is alive and talking to the cluster. The fault is between the port and the data wire, or in the tool. Go to Branch A.
  • YES, "no bus"The cluster is not hearing the PCM. Go to 1.2.
  • CLUSTER DEADNo gauges, no odometer at all. The cluster and the port share fuse 17 in the junction block. Go to A2 first.
1.2

Does the engine start and keep running?

A running engine proves the PCM has power, ground and a working brain. A no-start together with "no bus" usually means the PCM is not powered up at all. One special case: if the engine starts and dies after about two seconds, the Sentry Key immobilizer can't reach the PCM over the dead data wire. Treat that as "runs" for this question.

  • RUNS (or starts then dies at 2 s)The PCM works but nobody can hear it. The data wire is shorted or broken. Go to Branch C.
  • CRANKS, NO STARTCheck the PCM's own power and grounds first. Go to Branch E.
  • WON'T CRANKThe PCM does not control cranking on this truck. Fix the no-crank (battery, starter, neutral safety switch) separately, then come back and answer this question with the key in RUN.
Branch A

Check the port

Come here when the odometer is normal. The PCM is talking; the scanner just can't hear it. Three things have to be right at the port: power on pin 16, ground on pins 4 and 5, and a live signal on pin 2. Pin numbers are molded into the connector face. About 15 minutes.

A1

Is there power on pin 16?

Key in RUN. Red meter lead in pin 16, black lead in pin 4. Most WJs show this voltage with the key off too, but test with the key on.

Pin 16 to pin 4: battery voltage, about 12 to 12.6 V.0 V: no power. The scanner won't even light up.
  • 0 VGo to A2.
  • 12 VGo to A3.
A2

Check fuse 17 (10 amp) in the junction block

The junction block is the interior fuse box. Open the driver's door and pull the cover off the left end of the dash. Fuse 17 feeds both the port and the gauge cluster, which is why a dead cluster and a dead port happen together. Pull the fuse and look at it, or test both sides with a test light.

  • BLOWNReplace it, key on, retest A1. If it blows again, something is shorting that circuit: look at the port for a bent pin touching its neighbor, a crushed wire behind the port, or a damaged plug on the cluster.
  • GOOD, but still 0 V at pin 16The wire between fuse 17 and pin 16 is open, or the pin 16 terminal is corroded or pushed back out of the connector. Look at the back of the port: the pin 16 wire should be seated and clean. Repair, then retest A1.
A3

Are pins 4 and 5 good grounds?

Key in RUN. Black lead on the battery negative post (or a clean bolt on the body), red lead in pin 4, then pin 5. You are measuring how much voltage is lost across the ground wire. Both grounds run to the body ground behind the left kick panel.

Pin 4 or 5 to battery negative: under 0.2 V. (Or under 1 ohm with the battery disconnected.)
  • OVER 0.2 V / OPENClean or repair the kick panel ground and the pin terminals. Retest.
  • GOODGo to A4.
A4

Is the data wire alive at pin 2?

Key in RUN. Red lead in pin 2, black lead in pin 4. The data wire idles at 0 volts and pulses up to about 7.5 volts whenever any module talks. A multimeter averages those pulses, so a live bus shows a bouncing reading somewhere between about 1 and 3 volts. Turn the headlights or wipers on and off while watching: the reading should twitch.

Live: bouncing, roughly 1 to 3 V average.Steady 0 V: this branch of the wire is dead.Steady 12 V: the wire is touching battery power somewhere.
  • BOUNCINGThe port is fine and the data wire is live. Go to Branch D.
  • STEADY 0 VThe cluster hears the PCM but the port does not, so the short stub of wire from pin 2 to the rest of the bus is open or the pin 2 terminal is bad. Go to D2.
  • STEADY 12 VShorted to power. Go to Branch C.
Branch D

The tool, and the wire from the port to the PCM

Come here when the port has power, ground, and a live signal on pin 2. About 20 minutes.

D1

Does a second, known-good scan tool connect?

Borrow a wired handheld scanner (any parts store will plug one in for free). If your own tool is a phone app, this is also the moment to force the protocol to J1850 VPW instead of auto.

  • CONNECTSYour original tool is the problem. Done.
  • STILL NOTHINGGo to D2.
  • SEES ABS OR AIRBAG BUT NOT ENGINESome tools can list every module on the bus. If the tool reaches other modules but never the PCM, the PCM's own data pin or its internal bus driver is dead. Go to D3.
D2

Check the pin 2 terminal and the wire to the PCM

First look: shine a light into pin 2 and check that the terminal is not green, spread open, or pushed back. Then, with the battery disconnected, measure resistance from port pin 2 to the yellow-with-violet-stripe wire at the PCM's black connector (C1). Most WJ pinout charts put it at cavity 11 of C1; confirm on your wiring diagram. Backprobe from the wire side so you don't spread the terminal.

Port pin 2 to PCM data wire, battery off: under 1 ohm.
  • OPEN / HIGHBroken wire or bad terminal. The usual spots are behind the left kick panel where the harness bends, and any aftermarket splice. Repair and retest the scanner.
  • UNDER 1 OHMGo to D3.
D3 · End of branch

Everything measures right and two tools still can't reach the PCM

The engine runs, the cluster hears the PCM, the port is good, and the wire is continuous. What's left is inside the PCM: its bus transmitter has failed, or the PCM is so early that it needs a software update from a dealer-level tool before generic scanners can talk to it. Ask a dealer or a shop with a Chrysler DRB-III or wiTECH tool to try first; if their tool can't reach it either, replace the PCM (see E6 for what a replacement involves).

Branch C

The data wire is shorted or broken

Come here when the odometer says "no bus" but the engine runs, or when pin 2 sits at a steady 12 volts. Every module on the truck shares one wire. If one module fails internally and pins the wire to ground or to power, every module goes silent, including the PCM. The job is to find which one. Budget an hour.

C1

What is pin 2 doing?

Key in RUN, red lead in port pin 2, black lead in pin 4, same as A4.

Bouncing 1 to 3 V: the wire is live at the port.Steady 0 V: shorted to ground, or no module is transmitting.Steady 12 V: shorted to battery power.
  • BOUNCING, yet odometer says "no bus"The bus is alive but the cluster isn't hearing it. Suspect the cluster's own plug or the cluster itself. Pull the cluster, inspect its connector for a bent or backed-out data pin, reseat, retest. If "no bus" persists with a live wire, the cluster is at fault.
  • STEADY 0 V or 12 VGo to C2.
C2

Disconnect the battery and measure the wire's resistance to ground

Battery negative off. Ohmmeter from port pin 2 to pin 4. Each module hangs a resistor of roughly 10,000 ohms on the wire, so with eight or ten modules plugged in you should see a reading in the low thousands of ohms.

Normal: roughly 1,000 to 3,000 ohms.Near 0 ohms: hard short to ground somewhere.Very high / open: the wire is broken near the port (go back to D2).
  • NOTE THE READINGYou will watch this number (or the pin 2 voltage, or the odometer) change as you unplug modules. Go to C3.
C3

Unplug modules one at a time until "no bus" clears

For each module: battery off, unplug it, battery on, key RUN, wait 20 seconds, look at the odometer (or recheck pin 2). If "no bus" goes away, the module you just unplugged is the one dragging the wire down. Plug the previous one back in before moving to the next so you only ever have one module out. Work in this order, easiest and most common first:

  1. Radio. Pull it from the dash and unplug its connectors. A common bus killer on WJs.
  2. Overhead console (compass and trip computer). Drop it and unplug.
  3. ABS controller. Under the hood, driver's side, bolted to the brake hydraulic unit. Another frequent offender.
  4. Sentry Key immobilizer module, if equipped. The small module around the ignition lock cylinder.
  5. Airbag control module. Under the center console. Battery off and wait two minutes first.
  6. Automatic climate control head, memory seat module, driver and passenger door modules (Limited models).
  7. Junction block / body controller. The interior fuse box is also a computer. Unplug its large connectors.
  8. The PCM itself. Last, because it's the hardest to reach. Battery off, unplug all three connectors, battery on, key RUN. If "no bus" goes away (the cluster will now show "no bus" for a different reason, so watch pin 2 voltage return to normal instead), the PCM is shorting the bus.
  • CLEARS WHEN MODULE X IS OUTModule X is the fault. Before buying one, check its connector for water or corrosion, which can short the pins without the module being bad. Replace or repair, retest with the scanner. If X is the PCM, go to E6.
  • NEVER CLEARSGo to C4.
C4 · End of branch

No module clears it, so the wire itself is damaged

With every module unplugged the resistance from pin 2 to ground should be essentially infinite. If it still reads low, the insulation is rubbed through somewhere. Check these places, in order:

  • Behind the gauge cluster and under the left kick panel, where the harness bends sharply.
  • The grommet where the harness passes through the firewall on the driver's side.
  • The passenger-side cowl near the PCM. Leaves block the drains and water runs onto the PCM plugs and harness.
  • Any aftermarket splice, scotch-lock, or tapped wire from a stereo, alarm, or remote starter.
  • Under the hood near the ABS unit and along the inner fender.

Repair the damaged section with solder and heat shrink. Retest at C1.

Branch E

The PCM's own power, grounds and sensors

Come here when the engine cranks but won't start and the odometer says "no bus". A PCM with no power, no ground, or a shorted sensor supply never wakes up, so nothing can talk to it. About 45 minutes. The PCM is on the passenger-side firewall; the black connector (C1) carries the power and ground pins.

E1

Check the under-hood fuse box

The power distribution center is under the hood on the driver's side next to the battery. Read the lid map and check every fuse labeled for the PCM, engine controller, IOD (memory keep-alive), and the fuel pump / ASD (automatic shutdown) circuit, plus the ASD relay. Test each fuse with a test light on both sides rather than trusting your eyes.

  • BLOWNReplace, key RUN, retest the scanner. If it blows again there is a short on that circuit; a backwards jump start is the usual story and the PCM may be damaged.
  • ALL GOODGo to E2.
E2

Does the PCM have both of its power feeds?

On the black connector (C1), pins 2 and 22 are the two power feeds. One is constant battery power (keeps memory alive), the other is switched by the ignition. Backprobe each from the wire side with the connector plugged in; black lead on the battery negative post.

Key off: one of the two pins shows battery voltage.Key in RUN: both pins show battery voltage, about 12 V.
  • ONE OR BOTH MISSING KEY-ONIf the constant feed is missing, trace back to the under-hood fuse box. If only the switched feed is missing, the ignition switch or its wiring is the suspect; a worn WJ ignition switch is a known cause of dropped feeds. Also pull back the harness tape near the PCM and look for a chafed or aftermarket-tapped wire.
  • BOTH PRESENTGo to E3.
E3

Are the PCM grounds good?

Pins 31 and 32 on the black connector are the two ground wires (black with tan stripe). Key in RUN, red lead backprobing each pin, black lead on the battery negative post. These grounds run to the engine block; on the 4.0 the main engine ground is near the starter, and a braided strap ties the cylinder head to the firewall.

Pin 31 or 32 to battery negative, key on: under 0.1 V. (Battery off: under 1 ohm.)
  • OVER 0.1 V / OPENClean the engine ground near the starter, the head-to-firewall strap, and the battery negative connection at the fender. Repair the black/tan wires if damaged. Retest.
  • GOODGo to E4.
E4

Is the 5-volt sensor supply alive?

The PCM feeds 5 volts to its sensors. If any sensor shorts that supply to ground, the PCM shuts itself down to protect itself: no start, no communication, often "no bus". Key in RUN, backprobe the 5-volt wire at the throttle position sensor (TPS) on the throttle body; the wiring diagram shows which of its three wires is the supply (on most WJs it is the violet/white one).

TPS supply wire, key on: about 5.0 V.0 V: the supply is shorted or the PCM is not awake.
  • 0 VUnplug these one at a time, cycling the key between each: TPS, MAP sensor (on the throttle body), crankshaft position sensor (on the bellhousing), camshaft position sensor (in the distributor-style drive on the block), transmission governor pressure sensor (on the transmission), and the A/C pressure transducer. When the 5 volts comes back, the sensor you just unplugged is shorted; replace it and retest the scanner. If it never comes back with all of them unplugged, go to E5.
  • 5 V PRESENTGo to E5.
E5

Inspect the PCM connectors for water and corrosion

Battery off. Unplug all three PCM connectors (each has a lock lever or screw). Look at both the plug and the PCM side for green or white crust, water, bent pins, or terminals pushed back into the housing. The cowl drains sit right above this spot and clogged drains send water straight down onto the plugs. Clean with electrical contact cleaner, straighten pins, let everything dry, apply dielectric grease, reconnect firmly.

  • FOUND CORROSIONClean, reconnect, battery on, retest the scanner. If it now connects, clear the cowl drains so it doesn't come back.
  • CLEAN AND DRYGo to E6.
E6 · End of branch

Power, grounds, sensors and plugs all check out: the PCM has failed

This is the point where the evidence supports replacing the PCM. Two things to know first. These computers often fail from cracked solder joints inside, so a unit that works cold and drops out when the engine bay gets hot is also a PCM failure, and repair services exist for that. And a replacement is not plug-and-play: it must be programmed with the truck's VIN and mileage, and if the truck has Sentry Key it must be married to the key module, which takes a Chrysler-capable tool (dealer, mobile locksmith, or some independent shops). Remanufacturers can also ship one already programmed to your VIN. Before ordering, make sure branch C has been worked through too: a PCM with a shorted bus driver will still fail at C3 when unplugged, which confirms the diagnosis.

Modules on the data wire

Any one of these can silence all the others.

  • PCM (passenger firewall)
  • Gauge cluster
  • Junction block / body controller (left end of dash)
  • ABS controller (under hood, driver side, on the brake unit)
  • Airbag control module (under center console)
  • Radio
  • Overhead console
  • Sentry Key immobilizer (ignition lock), if equipped
  • Climate control head, memory seat module, door modules (Limited)

Where things are

  • Port: under the dash, left of the steering column.
  • Junction block (interior fuses): left end of the dash, behind the cover you see with the driver's door open.
  • Power distribution center (under-hood fuses, relays): driver's side of the engine bay beside the battery.
  • PCM: passenger side of the firewall, upper corner of the engine bay. Three plugs: black C1, white C2, gray C3.
  • PCM grounds: engine block near the starter, plus the braided head-to-firewall strap.

Readings at a glance

  • Battery: 12.4 V or more at rest.
  • Data wire, live: 0 V idle, 7.5 V pulses, reads 1 to 3 V bouncing on a meter.
  • Data wire, shorted: stuck at 0 V or stuck at 12 V.
  • Data wire to ground, battery off: roughly 1,000 to 3,000 ohms with everything plugged in.
  • Sensor supply at TPS: 5.0 V key on.
  • Any ground pin: under 0.1 to 0.2 V drop, under 1 ohm.

Most common endings, by how often they turn up

  1. Blown fuse 17 or a corroded pin 16 at the port (someone shorted the port).
  2. A scan tool or phone dongle that can't do J1850 VPW.
  3. A failed radio or ABS controller dragging the data wire down.
  4. Water in the PCM plugs from clogged cowl drains, or a shorted 5-volt sensor.
  5. The PCM itself, often heat-related solder cracks.

Fuse positions and wire colors are taken from WJ (1999 to 2004) fuse charts and service-manual pinouts shared by owners. Model-year wiring varies, so trust the printed fuse lid and your own wiring diagram over this sheet where they disagree. The PCM data pin is marked "verify" because owner-posted pinout charts were not conclusive for the 2000 4.0L.
